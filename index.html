The sortie

The first waves are a gentle warm-up; the Swarm sharpens up every wave. Shoot down a Warden carrying a captured fighter and that pilot joins you as a wingman for the rest of the wave, then flies home to the Halcyon as a spare fighter.

Every fifth wave is a boss, and there are three of them in rotation. A hive ship: your guns do nothing to its hull, so strip the four emitter pods, one on each face, and the core opens up — missiles hit a pod four times as hard as anything else. A hold-out: no capital ship, just an unending swarm, and the stage ends when Halcyon's relief flight of three arrives rather than when something dies. A Swarm ace: one elite pilot that out-turns you, breaks off three times to call escorts, and patches itself up while they live. Asteroids drift through every wave as cover, as a hazard, and as a bonus.

Each wave lists its objectives. They are bonuses, not conditions — you cannot fail one, and the only way to lose is to run out of fighters. Each one met raises that wave's score multiplier; clear them all and two extra missiles are loaded for the next wave.

The field carries space mines. A mine sits dark and stays off the radar until something comes within about 45 units — then it lights up, starts broadcasting, and follows whatever woke it for a couple of seconds before going off. It does not care whose ship it touches, so a Lancer led across one is a Lancer dealt with. Shoot one from outside the blast and it is simply gone, and a blast sets off its neighbours.

Supply crates restore shields or hull, or load two more missiles. They drift in the field, drop from Swarm wrecks — a Warden is worth about one in three — and are occasionally buried in an asteroid. What is inside leans towards whatever you are short of. Fly through one to take it.

Do not ram anything. A collision costs you far more than it costs them, and the heavy ships simply shrug it off and keep shooting.

Saving and the board

The run is saved between waves, so you can close the tab mid-sortie and pick the wave back up later, and the pause menu has a Save & quit button for leaving on purpose. Either way you resume at the start of the wave you left. Losing your last fighter ends the run and clears the save. The save lives in this browser only.

Launching goes full screen by default, because an address bar costs a quarter of the cockpit on a phone. Better still, install the game or add it to your home screen: it then opens as its own app with no browser around it at all, and it works with no connection. On iPhone and iPad that is the only way to be rid of the address bar, since Safari there has no full-screen mode for web pages.

There are two leader boards of twenty, three characters each: a global one on the game's own server, and a local one kept in this browser for when the server cannot be reached. A finished run is written to the local board first and sent to the global one afterwards, so a score is never lost to a timeout.

What the global board records

Posting a score sends the run's numbers — score, wave, kills, shots, hits, asteroids, how long it took — plus your three characters and your device's time zone and local clock.

The server additionally records, from the request itself: your IP address, the country, region and city that address resolves to, your browser user-agent string, and the operating system, browser and device type read out of it. These are kept to spot forged scores and to see what the game is being played on. Only the name, score, wave, date and country are ever shown to anyone; the rest is never served back.

The lookup from IP to place is done by ip-api.com, which means your IP address is sent to that service and cached here for a month. Nothing is recorded unless you confirm a name on the debrief screen — play without entering one and nothing about you is stored. Playing offline, or with the server unreachable, uses the local board and sends nothing at all.

What is shown publicly is on the records page: the board, a country-level map, and which platforms the game is being played on. The rest is visible only to the game's administrator.

Built with

three.js, and nothing else at runtime. No build step and no server: one page plus generated assets is the whole game. Every sound, voice line and texture is synthesised rather than recorded — the source explains how.

Ship models: Quaternius Ultimate Spaceships · surface textures: ambientCG · both CC0
